During my undergraduate studies in Industrial Engineering, I took some subjects I didn’t enjoy at all and others I felt more affinity with, but the one I connected with immediately was statistics. The way it allows us to understand the world through probabilities and its applications in different scientific and life contexts deeply caught my attention. In addition to this, I have always felt drawn to teaching, as I believe it’s one of the most beautiful and efficient ways to contribute to society—by sharing knowledge and influencing the professionals of tomorrow. With my personal aspirations and professional goals in mind, I decided to enroll in the Master's in Applied Statistics at Universidad del Norte.

Over these two years, I have undergone both a personal and professional transformation. I entered the program eager to deepen my knowledge of statistical tools to solve real-world problems, and I leave with a more critical, rigorous, and passionate view of data analysis, along with the future goal of becoming a research professor. My research line focused on Topic Modelling, particularly the **Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA)** model. This choice was born from my interest in discovering hidden patterns and topics in large volumes of text—something increasingly relevant in the age of unstructured data. As part of my thesis, I developed the guide <a href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/388458969_A_Comprehensive_Guide_to_Latent_Dirichlet_Allocation_Building_a_Solid_Foundation_with_Key_Statistical_Concepts?_tp=eyJjb250ZXh0Ijp7InBhZ2UiOiJwcm9maWxlIiwicHJldmlvdXNQYWdlIjoiaG9tZSIsInBvc2l0aW9uIjoicGFnZUNvbnRlbnQifX0" style="text-decoration: none;">***"A Comprehensive Guide to Latent Dirichlet Allocation: Building a Solid Foundation with Key Statistical Concepts"*** (See here)</a>, where complex theoretical concepts such as Bayesian inference, Gibbs sampling, and Dirichlet distributions are broken down and linked to the practical implementation of the model. This work, besides being an academic contribution, allowed me to strengthen skills in technical writing, R and Python programming, and results visualization. I also had the opportunity to apply these skills in real-world settings, such as text analysis involving the Marshall-Olkin Bivariate distribution and consulting projects in the port sector, where I integrated machine learning and multivariate analysis.

My time in the master’s program wasn’t just technical. I also served as the student representative on the curriculum committee, which gave me an insider view of the collective construction of the program and allowed me to develop academic leadership skills.  
Today, I look back with gratitude for every challenge, every study night, every class, and for the support of professors and classmates who always strived for excellence. This experience has profoundly shaped my career, opened new doors, and consolidated me as a professional with a solid foundation in statistics, equipped with tools that will surely help me achieve my goals.
